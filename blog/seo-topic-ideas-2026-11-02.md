# SEO page ideas from this week's creator videos

Week of Oct 26 – Nov 2, 2026. Latest long-form YouTube uploads from 10 creators whose audience is business owners.

**How this was made:** every summary comes from the video's full transcript (pulled with Supadata), not from titles or descriptions. Nothing was added that the creator didn't say. Stats and claims are **the creator's own**: double-check any number before putting it on a TAAM page and cite the creator as the source. Keywords are suggestions and haven't been checked for volume yet.

## Themes across this week's videos

**1. Everything sounds the same now, and being human and specific wins.** Five videos made this point on their own:
- **Neil Patel:** human content was 14.5% of what 100 companies published but brought in 87% of their traffic.
- **Codie Sanchez:** "average is now the cheapest thing you can be because a machine makes it for free."
- **MATG (books episode):** AI is commoditizing marketing, so go back to the classics.
- **MATG with Ruben Hassid:** the best AI writers stopped using AI to write, because "writing is thinking."
- **GaryVee:** the content itself decides reach now, not your follower count.

**2. Stop competing on price and selling to everyone.** Priestley (price maker vs price taker) and Hormozi (most owners are "underpriced trying to sell to everyone") agree here. This fits TAAM's no-discount rule.

**3. Make the format fit the attention span.** Ben Heath's data shows static image ads overtaking video, and Sophie Miller (HubSpot) shows how to turn one idea into four post formats.

## 1. Alex Hormozi: "Change My Mind, Win $10,000"
https://youtu.be/Uzr0gv4N4-s · 43 min

**What it is:** a debate format. Business owners try to argue Hormozi out of his tweets, and the best counterargument wins $10K.

**What was actually said:**
- **"You don't need a weekend."** Hormozi's point is that needing one means you hate your work. Someone working weekends gets "104 more work days per year," and speed is "probably the single greatest differentiator between businesses."
- **"Cut three B players, hire one A player."** Pay the A player what you'd pay two B players. A guest pushed back that early-stage businesses often can't afford A players.
- **"Sell to the rich until you can sell to the poor."** Hormozi: "nine times out of 10 when I'm talking to a business owner, they're probably underpriced trying to sell to everyone rather than picking [a] specific avatar that has the most gross profit." A low-cost leader has to make it up in volume, which needs more capital up front.
- **"You lack priorities, not information."** Most owners directionally know what to do. It's a behavior problem, not an information problem.
- **"Profit is overrated."** Hormozi's correction: losing money is only fine with a clear return-on-investment thesis. Many owners "say they are reinvesting in growth when the reality is they just don't make any money."
- **Result:** he split the $10K. He conceded "never" was wrong on work-life balance (Patagonia's founder as the counterexample). He also conceded his core audience of business owners is already trying to be exceptional.

**SEO pages:**
- **"Are you underpriced? Why selling to everyone is costing you"** Target: `how to know if you are underpriced`, `stop selling to everyone`, `ideal customer avatar small business`
- **"Pick a niche: why the most profitable customer beats the most customers"** Target: `how to choose a target market small business`

## 2. Neil Patel: "How to Use AI Without Getting Flagged as Slop"
https://youtu.be/7qOP30htmNY · 11 min

**What was actually said:**
- **His team's data across 100 companies:** AI content was 52.1% of what they published but brought in 4.9% of organic traffic. Human content was 14.5% of what they published and brought in 87% of traffic.
- **Graphite studies:** 49.9% of new articles were mostly AI-written, yet 86% of articles showing up in Google search were human-written.
- **Google's March 2024 "scaled content abuse" policy:** Google doesn't need to prove AI wrote something, only that pages are published faster than anyone could make them useful. Fifty generic pages on one topic compete with each other and can drag down your one good page.
- **Platforms are filtering it out:**
  - LinkedIn (May): pulling generic AI posts "with no clear point of view" out of recommendations. In July it added a button to report AI slop.
  - Snap stopped recommending fully AI videos on Spotlight.
  - YouTube (July 2025): mass-produced repetitive videos can't be monetized.
  - LinkedIn's feed uses dwell time as a signal.
- **People react to the label:** in a 696-person study, the same Instagram post got less engagement when labeled AI. He calls this the "effort heuristic."
- **Social data:** human posts average 15.7 likes vs 7.3 for fully AI posts, and get saved about 3x as often. AI drafts edited by a human averaged 6.8.
- **744-article test over 12 months:** human articles got 5.4x more traffic per post by month 5, and the gap held through month 12.
- **His test:** "Could a competitor with the same AI have published it?" If yes, it's slop. Add your own data, a real result with a number, a named customer story, or a failed test.
- **His weekly plan:**
  - Monday: note one real thing that happened in your business.
  - Wednesday: write it up yourself, with a number in it.
  - Friday: publish it.
  - Measure sales, replies and time on page, not impressions. "One post a week instead of five" will usually beat the other five combined.

**SEO pages:**
- **Pillar: "Is AI content hurting your business? What the data says"** Target: `ai content vs human content`, `does ai content rank on google`, `ai generated content seo`
- **"Why your AI blog posts aren't ranking (scaled content abuse explained)"** Target: `scaled content abuse`, `google ai content penalty`
- **"Publish less, rank more: a weekly content plan for business owners"** Target: `how often should a small business post`, `content strategy for small business`

## 4. Ben Heath: "This new Facebook Ads trend is crazy..."
https://youtu.be/2VWnj9C9PvQ · 16 min

**The trend:** static image ads are now beating video ads, reversing about a decade of video winning.

**What was actually said:**
- **His audience polls** on which ad format works best:
  - 4 years ago: video 44%, image 38%
  - 3 years ago: video 53%
  - 2 years ago: video 60%, image 27%
  - **3 days before recording: static 54%, video 38%, other 8%**
- **His agency's own data:** about $100M in spend last year across hundreds of clients. Meta is putting more and more budget into static ads.
- **His reasons:** attention spans are shrinking, and drop-off on video ads is high ("is your drop off rate 90%? That wouldn't be unusual"). Statics also stand out now that most of the feed is video.
- **What to do:**
  - Don't make only statics. Different people respond to different formats, and Meta sequences ads (for example video for awareness, statics for retargeting).
  - Statics need sharper, persona-specific messaging (his example: running shorts for sub-zero, long-distance runners).
  - AI-looking static ads "don't perform well at all." AI product photos that look like a real shoot are fine.
  - **Video still wins** for brand-new concepts that need explaining, and for expertise-based services sold to cold audiences. Warm audiences who already know you do well with statics.
  - Test both, then lean into the winner without dropping the other.
- **His prediction:** "animated statics" (a static image with slight motion, often made with AI) become a top performer.

**Why it matters for TAAM:** we sell video, so be honest here. By his own reasoning, video is still the right call for expertise-based local services and cold audiences. The best page is a balanced one, which builds trust.

**SEO pages:**
- **"Image ads vs video ads on Facebook: which should your business run?"** Target: `image vs video ads facebook`, `best facebook ad format`, `static ads vs video ads`
- **"Facebook ads for Las Vegas businesses"** (local service page) Target: `facebook ads agency las vegas`, `meta ads las vegas`

## 5. HubSpot Marketing: "How To Create a Social Content Calendar You'll Actually Stick To"
https://youtu.be/wmkUM2Edm6A · 11 min

**Who's in it:** Sophie Miller (Pretty Little Marketer): 620K LinkedIn followers, 190K Instagram, 20K newsletter readers and 600 paying members, all organic. This is part 2 of a 4-part series.

**What was actually said:**
- **The system:** mission → **pillars** (what you cover) × **formats** (the shape it comes out in) = your calendar. "You're picking a box and you're filling it."
- **Pillars:** derive them from your mission by asking "what would I need to post to deliver that mission to those people?" Aim for 4 (3 is fine, more than 5 is too many). Her 4: *Overhyped or underrated*, *The foundations*, *Working as a team of one*, *How the industry really works*.
- **The order:** pillar → opinion → format. "If you pick the format first, you end up writing a carousel about something that needed to be one line."
- **Her 4 formats,** shown with one idea (AI-generated comments):
  - **Thin piece:** short, makes people feel seen
  - **Explainer:** teaches, gets saved
  - **Experience:** your own story, can't be copied
  - **Observation:** your take, gets argued with
- **Two audiences:** *community* (peers, built for reach and conversation, no pitch) vs *client* (who you sell to). One post can do both.
- **Tools:** Notion for planning, Planable for Instagram scheduling, LinkedIn's native scheduler. Batching: "Pick a morning, write three, schedule them, and you're done."
- **Three habits:** start wide then go specific; give the why or the how; be a team player in other people's comments.

**SEO pages:**
- **"Social media content calendar for small business (free template)"** Target: `social media content calendar template`, `content calendar for small business`. Could also become a free tool in `/tools`.
- **"Content pillars for business owners: how to pick yours (with examples)"** Target: `content pillars examples`, `what to post on social media for business`
- **"One idea, four posts: how to never run out of content"** Target: `content ideas for small business`, `repurpose content ideas`

## 6. Codie Sanchez: "It's Never Been Easier To Be In The Top 1%"
https://youtu.be/1hk_VMEeoPQ · 13 min

**What was actually said:**
- **Everything is converging on average:** identical Airbnbs (writer Kyle Chayka calls it "AirSpace," from his book *Filterworld*), coffee shops, fonts and logos. Algorithms show what already performed well.
- **A UCLA/Exeter study:** writers who used AI ideas wrote stories that scored higher individually, but collectively they "all sounded exactly the same."
- **"Original is expensive again because it's scarce again."** You don't need a genius idea, just an original one: "say the thing many people are thinking and nobody wants to say out loud."
- **Be deep, not wide.** The winners are extreme experts or extreme generalists (the founder profile). Most people are "too wide and not deep."
- **Copy principles, not solutions:**
  - Liquid Death worked because of *identity marketing* (punk fans who didn't want to be seen with plain water), not the skull on the can.
  - Amazon works because people are impatient.
  - Raising Cane's works because people are indecisive: fewer options, done really well.
- **Rules vs laws:** laws end your business if you break them. Rules are just agreements you can break.
- **Three kinds of controllable luck:** volume ("ass in the chair"), going where the action is, and reputation.
- **The "creativity death spiral":** success leads to protecting what you have, which means less risk and less growth (BlackBerry). Her fix is a 70/20/10 split: 70% proven, 20% variations on what's proven, 10% wild card.

**SEO pages:**
- **"How to make your local business stand out when everyone looks the same"** Target: `how to make your business stand out`, `brand differentiation small business`
- **"Identity marketing: what Liquid Death can teach a local business"** Target: `identity marketing examples`, `liquid death marketing strategy`
- Feeds the AI-sameness pillar page.

## 7. GaryVee: "The Biggest Social Media Shift Nobody Is Talking About"
https://youtu.be/aP7msE4tJqU · 6 min (a clip from the Social Currency podcast)

**What was actually said:**
- **Social media has become "interest media":** you get content based on your behavior, not who you follow. He traces it from Tumblr to Musical.ly to TikTok.
- **"Someone today with no following can put out a piece of content that outperforms someone who has millions of followers."** Followers still give "some sort of inherent opportunity for more reach," but "it's just the content."

- **Every business is a media company first,** "whether they're a SaaS B2B business or they sell concrete or they sell flowers." How to start: pick your medium (writing, audio or video) and make content. Interviewing people makes it easier.

## 8. Marketing Against the Grain: "100+ Years of Marketing Advice in 26 Minutes"
https://youtu.be/1_Lh95jRWDY · 26 min

**Who's in it:** Kipp Bodnar and Kieran Flanagan (HubSpot).

**What was actually said:**
- **Their premise:** "All of marketing is becoming commoditized by AI." Endless tactical tutorials are "the marketing hamster wheel."
- **The books and their one lesson each:**
  - **Herschell Gordon Lewis, *Direct Mail Copy That Sells*:** verisimilitude. "The cornerstone of successful direct response copy isn't truth, but having the reader regard what you write as truth." Copy has to *feel* true.
  - **W.I.B. Beveridge, *The Art of Scientific Investigation*:** "Originality often consists in linking up ideas whose connection was not previously suspected." Take inputs from other industries (they cite Liquid Death again).
  - **Rosser Reeves, *Reality in Advertising*:** it's easy to grow an audience and hard to grow *the right* audience. Reeves wrote M&M's "melts in your mouth, not in your hands," which sells the benefit, not the product.
  - **Seth Godin, *Tribes*:** "The enemy is the middle," not the opposite. Be for specific people and accept that you're not for others.
  - **Dale Carnegie, *How to Win Friends and Influence People*:** no one cares about your product. Show them the person they want to become.
  - **Robert Updegraff, *Obvious Adams*:** a good idea "explodes" in people's minds. If it takes lengthy explanation, it isn't obvious yet, or you haven't simplified it. They call this positioning and product marketing.
- **Kieran is moving toward "no AI at all"** in his content, not even for ideas, because everyone ends up in the same place.
- **The oldest ad they found:** a Pompeii wine seller with three price tiers (1, 2 and 4 coins). "Do you know what you're getting, why it's valuable to you, and what you need to exchange for it."

- **"Best marketing books for business owners (and the one lesson from each)"** Target: `best marketing books for small business owners`, `marketing books for entrepreneurs`
- **"Good, better, best: how to structure your service packages"** (from the Pompeii tiers, fits the no-discount rule) Target: `good better best pricing`, `tiered pricing for service business`

## 10. Daniel Priestley: "If You Don't Understand Microeconomics, You Don't Understand Business"
https://youtu.be/cZSkucl4Png · 33 min

**What was actually said:**
- **"Every business is either a price taker or a price maker."** A price taker lets the market set its price. A price maker decides its worth and the market pays it.
- **Law 1, ignore the macro:** tune out the news and focus only on what you control (your offer, positioning, niche, campaigns, ideal customer).
- **Law 2, supply and demand:** your price has nothing to do with how good you are or how hard you work. Most owners have a *supply* mindset (better product, longer hours, more staff), which raises supply and pushes prices down. Switch to *demand generation*, and create visible "demand and supply tension." His example: the ice cream truck that announces limited stock before opening.
- **Law 3, build your own market:** sell in "ring-fenced environments" where buyers can't easily compare you (Apple Stores don't show Samsung prices).
  - Give your service its own name.
  - Never list on comparison marketplaces.
  - His business coach example: rename the coaching, speak at an event of 200–300 CEOs, offer only 12 spots a year. Over 100 people scanned the QR code.
- **Law 4, here and now:** busy, wealthy buyers pay more for an offer in front of them now than for a cheaper one they'd have to go find (the $100 Bentley wash at the door). Use real scarcity: limited capacity, or a time limit on the offer.
- **Law 5, utility:** customers don't pay for your effort, they pay for what it does for them. Value is "90% dependent upon who's looking."
  - Esther Perel sells couples therapy to billionaires facing an expensive divorce.
  - "Turning screw $1. Knowing which screw to turn, $9,999."
  - Sell to people while they're still problem-aware, before they're comparing solutions.
- **Bring it together through campaigns:** a ring-fenced audience, value framed as utility, a here-and-now offer and constrained supply.

**SEO pages (strong fit for TAAM's no-discount positioning):**
- **"How to charge more than your competitors (without lowering your price to compete)"** Target: `how to charge premium prices`, `price maker vs price taker`, `stop competing on price`
- **"Why you should never list your business on comparison sites"** Target: `are lead marketplaces worth it`, `thumbtack alternatives for contractors`
- **"Sell the result, not the hours: pricing for service businesses"** Target: `value based pricing for service business`
