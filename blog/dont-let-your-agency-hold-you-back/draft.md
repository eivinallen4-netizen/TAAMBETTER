# Don't Let Your Marketing Agency Hold You Back

You already did the hard part. You built a business people love once they find it. So if growth has stalled, it's worth asking the honest question: is your marketing agency holding you back? Or is it the employee you handed social media to three years ago?

We can't make you good at what you do. You already did that. Our job is to show it to the people who need to see it. And when your marketing isn't doing that, it doesn't matter how long the relationship has lasted.

## How do you know your marketing agency is holding you back?

Your agency is holding you back when your business is growing faster than your marketing is. If the reports look the same every month, the content looks the same every year, and nobody can tell you in real numbers what winning looks like, that's your answer.

A few more signs to watch for:

- **Same report, different date.** The numbers move a little, the advice never changes.
- **You can't remember their last big idea.** Every new thing they've done, you had to ask for.
- **Your posts could belong to anyone.** Swap your logo for a competitor's and nobody would notice.
- **"Engagement" is the headline.** Calls, bookings and sales never come up.
- **You're doing the creative thinking.** You're paying a retainer and still writing the captions in your head.

Here's the side-by-side:

| What you've got now | What better marketing looks like |
|---|---|
| The same monthly report | Goals set in real numbers before anything gets made |
| Stock photos and templates | Your real team, filmed where the work happens |
| A few posts a week, when someone has time | A steady stream of short and long-form video, every month |
| Ideas only when you ask | A team that's hungry for your next big problem |
| Likes as the win | Calls, bookings and sales as the win |

## Why do businesses stay with an agency that isn't working?

Mostly because it's comfortable. After ten years the agency has your logins, your logo files and your habits, and switching feels like more work than staying.

But comfortable isn't the same as good. Loyalty is a great quality in a person. In a vendor, it can quietly cost you the goals you set for your company.

## What about the employee you handed social media to?

Handing social to someone on your team is how most businesses start, and there's nothing wrong with that. The problem is when it never grows up: they're doing it between their real job, without a plan, a camera crew or anyone telling them what's working.

That's not their fault. Creative that makes your customers say WOW takes strategy, shooting, editing and posting on a schedule, every single week. That's a job, not a side task.

Better marketing in 2026 means video, made consistently, starring your real people. Short-form is where most of that happens. ## How do you switch marketing agencies without losing momentum?

Switch in order: get your accounts and files in your own name first, agree on goals in real numbers with the new team, then give your old agency notice. Most of the pain of switching comes from doing those steps backwards.

1. **Own your stuff.** Make sure your ad accounts, social logins, domain, website hosting and raw footage are in your name, not theirs.
2. **Read your contract.** Know your notice period before you have the conversation.
3. **Decide what winning looks like.** Real numbers, agreed before the new team makes a single thing.
4. **Overlap if you can.** A short handoff beats a gap where nothing gets posted.

## How long until you see results from a new agency?

Content compounds, so give it a few months to show what it can do. Decide what winning looks like in real numbers on day one, so you're judging results instead of vibes.
