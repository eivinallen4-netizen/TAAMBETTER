# Is the Friend Doing Your Social Media Holding Your Business Back?

We see it all the time. A great business, with great people and a product customers love, is stuck. Not because the work isn't good, but because the friend doing their social media has been doing it for years and nobody wants to have that conversation. Or because the owner is scared to see what another agency would do, and what it might say about the last few years.

We get it. But you didn't build something this good to keep it a secret.

## Why do so many businesses let a friend run their social media?

Because it's easy and it's personal. You trust them, they already know the business, and nobody wants an awkward conversation with someone they care about.

And to be fair, it usually starts as a great favor. Someone who "knows Instagram" offers to help, posts start going up, and it feels handled. The problem is that years later it's still a favor, still squeezed in when they have time, and nobody's checking whether it's actually growing the business.

## How do you know the friend doing your social media isn't working out?

Look at what the posts actually bring in, not how they look. If your social hasn't brought in calls, bookings or sales you can point to, it's a favor, not marketing.

A few honest questions to ask yourself:

- **Is there a plan?** Or does something go up when someone remembers?
- **Is there video?** Real video of your real team, made every week?
- **Do you know what's working?** If nobody can tell you which posts brought in customers, nobody's steering.
- **Would you hire them today?** If you were starting fresh, would you pick this setup?
- **Are you avoiding the question?** If thinking about it makes you uncomfortable, that's an answer too.

## Why are business owners scared to talk to another agency?

Because not knowing feels safer than finding out. People naturally stick with what they already have, even when a better option is sitting right in front of them.

That's not just us saying it. In their classic study on decision making, economists William Samuelson and Richard Zeckhauser found that [individuals disproportionately stick with the status quo](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/jrisku/v1y1988i1p7-59.html), even on decisions as big as health plans and retirement savings. Your marketing is no different.

## What does it cost to keep things the way they are?

You never get a bill for the customers who didn't find you. That's what makes staying put feel free when it isn't.

The people you want are already watching video. [85% of people say they've been convinced to buy something by watching a video](https://wyzowl.com/video-marketing-statistics/), and [63% would rather learn about a product from a short video](https://wyzowl.com/video-marketing-statistics/) than any other way. If your social is a few photos a month, they're watching someone else's.

## How do you tell a friend you're moving your social media to an agency?

Tell them straight, thank them for real, and make it about the goals of the business instead of the quality of their work. A good friend wants you to win more than they want the login.

1. **Get the logins first.** Make sure every account, password and file is in your business's name.
2. **Have the conversation in person.** Not a text.
3. **Thank them for what they built.** They helped when you needed it. Say so.
4. **Keep the friendship.** Dinner's still on. The social media just has a new job description.
